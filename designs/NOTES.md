# What good posters actually look like: notes from looking at real ones

Looked at in image search on 2026-11-16: 100 Beste Plakate winners, Josef Müller-Brockmann and Armin Hofmann, Pentagram / Paula Scher (Public Theater, London Design Festival), Experimental Jetset, "letter O" typographic posters, circle-led typographic posters. These are descriptions of what is on the sheets, to build from. Nothing here is to be copied; the moves are what matter.

## The theme we are designing in
The person's words: "I just need theme of Optum, not their posters" and "maybe big O is their theme". So:
- **The Big O**: a thick ring, the letter O. It is the hero of every sheet, not a frame for an icon.
- **Palette**: warm white #FAF8F2, charcoal #3D3C38, orange #FF612B, pale sky #DFF3F9, deep teal #095F87 for tiny labels. Tints of orange are allowed where two orange shapes overlap (a deeper orange).
- **Two typefaces only**: Figtree (a grotesque sans, weights 300 to 900) and Newsreader (a serif, upright and italic).
- No drawn people, no icons, no pictograms. They are what made the last version look generated.

## Moves seen in real posters

1. **One letter is the whole poster.** The "Ö" posters: a single black O so large it leaves the sheet on three sides; the only other things are two dots and a caption in 8pt type in one corner. The counter (the hole) is the empty space. Nothing is centred; the O sits low and left or is cut by the right edge.

2. **Discs on a diagonal, text as a grid at the foot.** Müller-Brockmann, "musica viva": three or four black discs of falling size stepping down a diagonal on a pale ground, taking the top two thirds. All the text is tiny, in three or four narrow flush-left columns along the bottom, on a strict grid. The biggest disc is about 55% of the sheet width; the text is under 2% of the sheet height.

3. **One disc, one dot.** Müller-Brockmann, "Anthologie de musique suisse": one orange disc about 60% of the sheet width on a near-black ground with a small black dot at its centre (a record). The text is a narrow centred column of small type running down from the disc. Two colours and a pale band at the bottom.

4. **Concentric arcs that grow.** Müller-Brockmann, Beethoven: white arcs of increasing thickness sweeping round a centre on black, like sound. The text is a tiny block at the centre of the arcs. The arcs leave the sheet.

5. **A big disc and a bold flush-left block.** Hofmann / "Eidg. Schützenfest": a black disc (a target) filling the lower two thirds, and three lines of heavy red sans at the top left, flush left, set tight. Nothing else.

6. **Type fills the sheet; words break where the sheet ends.** Experimental Jetset: heavy grotesque set so large that each line fills the full width and words break mid-word across lines ("INTER / NET / PAINT / INGS"), leading tighter than the cap height, the type cut by the edges. One colour of type, small text in the gaps. "Caged& / Abused& / Gassed& / Skinned& / Worn." is five lines, flush left, filling the lower two thirds, with small text top left.

7. **Text set in rings.** Paula Scher, Public Theater "Twelfth Night": words set on concentric circles, ring inside ring, like a target made of type. Bands of rotated type at the edges.

8. **Numerals as architecture.** Pentagram, "LDF 2020"; 100 Beste Plakate "100": numbers set so large and tight that they become blocks filling the sheet edge to edge, stacked, sometimes rotated 90 degrees. The information is tiny, tucked into a corner or a cut-out rectangle.

9. **A cluster of overlapping discs plus a giant date.** A club poster ("16 / Ds16.03.19"): two colours only (pink ground, red ink). A cluster of four or five overlapping red discs forms one lumpy shape in the middle; the date "16" is set huge at the top right, bleeding off; the names are set large at the bottom edge; small details in a column at the left.

10. **Interlocking quarter and half circles.** "Bauhaus Ausstellung"-style: big quarter circles and half circles in three flat colours lock together like tiles and fill the top two thirds; a plain ground strip at the bottom holds the title in small caps and the date.

11. **Radial strokes and rows of text.** "Spasimo Palermo / VB62": a burst of short straight strokes radiating round a centre, on white, off to the right; the text is four short lines of bold sans spaced far apart down the left edge, one per row, like a list of facts given equal weight.

## What they all share
- **Two or three flat colours.** No gradients, no shadows, no outlines used as decoration.
- **A size ratio of 20:1 or more** between the biggest thing and the smallest text. Small text is properly small (1.5 to 2% of the sheet height) and set in tight flush-left columns on a grid.
- **Something leaves the sheet.** The biggest element is cropped by at least one edge.
- **Asymmetry.** The mass is in one corner or along one edge; the text is in the opposite one.
- **No labels, no pills, no icons, no "When / Time / Where" strip across the bottom.** Facts are set as plain lines of type in a column.
- **One idea you can say in a sentence**: "the O is a record", "the type is the building", "the discs are getting quieter".

## What the last version got wrong (the person said it "looks AI")
- Pictogram people and an icon inside a circle.
- A pill-shaped label top left on every sheet.
- A thin outline circle used as decoration.
- The same skeleton every time: label, headline, picture, strip of facts.
- Safe sizes: the headline was about 8 times the small text, not 20 times, and nothing was cropped hard.
- The facts in a pale band with three labelled boxes, like a form.

## Social posts by companies whose whole theme is a circle (looked at 2026-11-16)

The person's words: "poster for socmed posting", "like for a company". So the main sizes are the 4:5 feed post (1080 x 1350) and the square (1080 x 1080), then the 9:16 story.

12. **The brand shape replaces letters.** Headspace (Italic Studio): "Watch this sp●●ce." set in heavy sans on a flat orange ground, the two letters replaced by the brand's dots. The words are the whole post; the shape is inside the words.

13. **One flat saturated ground, one sentence, the shape.** Headspace: "Say hello to Headspace" in white, flush left, four lines, on solid orange; nothing else. Or a flat yellow ground with the orange dot dead centre and nothing else. Full-bleed colour, not a white card with a coloured corner.

14. **The shape becomes the thing.** Headspace: the dot is the sun rising over a hill, the head of a sitter. Mastercard: two overlapping circles become two umbrellas from above, the two cups of a pair of headphones, two stamps, two hats, and the logo is never shown. One circle, used as the subject, is the whole idea.

15. **Text on the circle.** Headspace and a "Circle" identity on Behance: a short phrase set round the outside of the disc on a circular path ("meet. create. connect."), reading clockwise, with the disc empty or holding one thing.

16. **A grid of the shape.** The "Circle" identity: nine discs in a 3 by 3 grid filling the square post; or one disc so large it is cropped by three edges with the wordmark tiny in a corner; the same system in red, black, blue and white tiles so a feed of posts reads as one family.

17. **Type as specimen.** Pentagram for Pfizer: a post that is only one giant glyph ("5", "P") in the brand colour on a pale tint, or five words stacked in alternating upright and italic, filling the tile edge to edge. No picture at all.

## Templates to stay away from (what "we're hiring" template sites look like)
Pill buttons that say "Apply now", rounded boxes with dashed borders, chips with icons, a contact strip with phone and web icons, a megaphone or a 3D "HIRING" word, a stock photo in a circle with a ring round it, five fonts. If a post has more than one box with a border, it is already this.
